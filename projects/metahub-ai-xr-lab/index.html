KU MetaHub AI/XR Lab

Lab-specialist work turning an immersive AI/XR facility into a usable research and teaching environment.

Role
Lab operations, facility readiness, vendor coordination, adoption support
Methods
AI/XR systems, 360 projection, AR/VR, haptics, control-room workflows
Status
Current full-time responsibility

The problem

What had to change

High-end AI/XR facilities can look impressive while still being fragile in daily use. The practical problem is turning equipment, vendors, rooms, users, and expectations into a dependable operating environment.

Constraints

The shape of the work

  • Multiple technical systems have to work together: projection, AR/VR, haptics, analytics, control-room workflows, and support infrastructure.
  • Researchers, educators, vendors, and facility users each describe readiness in different language.
  • Adoption depends on repeatable routines, not one-off demonstrations.

System

From facility scope to usable lab

  1. 01

    Scope and inventory

    Map room capabilities, handover items, risks, stakeholders, and expected use cases.

  2. 02

    Readiness routines

    Convert technical dependencies into checks, scheduling patterns, escalation paths, and training needs.

  3. 03

    User adoption

    Support researchers and educators so the technology becomes a stable environment rather than the main friction.

Proof & artifacts

What remains inspectable

Readiness checklists

Operational routines for room preparation, equipment availability, vendor follow-up, and handover gaps.

Stakeholder map

Translation layer across vendors, researchers, educators, students, and facility operators.

Workflow diagrams

Practical flows for demos, sessions, troubleshooting, training, and control-room coordination.

Adoption notes

Field observations on what makes immersive infrastructure trustworthy in repeated use.

Decisions

Choices that shaped the result

  • Treat the lab as decision infrastructure, not as a showroom.
  • Prioritize boring reliability before high-visibility demonstrations.
  • Capture handover and training needs as operating artifacts, not informal memory.

Outcomes

What the work made possible

  • Clearer readiness language across technical and non-technical stakeholders.
  • More inspectable operating routines for immersive teaching and research sessions.
  • Stronger bridge between facility capability and actual user confidence.

The KU MetaHub is an immersive learning and research facility built around AI, XR, 360-degree projection, AR/VR, haptics, analytics, and control-room workflows.

My role is to make the facility operationally real. That means translating scope into usable practice: readiness checks, equipment coordination, vendor follow-up, training needs, scheduling patterns, technical handover, and the routines researchers and educators need before a lab becomes dependable.

Narrative

High-end lab infrastructure only matters when people can trust it. The MetaHub work is about reliability, translation, and adoption: making sure a technically complex facility becomes a research environment rather than a showroom.

The useful work is often quiet: a readiness check that catches a missing dependency, a vendor note that closes a handover gap, a session plan that makes a room easier to teach in, or a training path that lets people use the technology without treating every session as a special event.

What This Shows

  • Operating judgment around emerging technology infrastructure.
  • The ability to turn vendor handover and facility complexity into repeatable routines.
  • A practical bridge between researchers, educators, technical teams, and users.
